# ATBC Study Group, 1994. The effect of vitamin E and beta carotene on the incidence of lung cancer and other cancers in male smokers. New England Journal of Medicine.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8127329/
DOI: 10.1056/NEJM199404143301501
Used for: Daily beta-carotene 20 mg increased lung-cancer incidence 18% and total mortality 8% in male smokers — established harm of an antioxidant sold as DNA protection.
Mark: ☠︎︎
N / effect / population / endpoint / duration: 29,133 male smokers 50–69, southwestern Finland; 2×2 factorial: α-tocopherol 50 mg/d, beta-carotene 20 mg/d, both, or placebo; 5–8 years. 876 lung cancers. Beta-carotene: +18% lung cancer (95% CI 3–36%); +8% total mortality (95% CI 1–16%, P=0.02). α-tocopherol: lung cancer −2% (95% CI −14 to +12%), not significant.
Conflict if any: Public-health trial (Finland/NCI). Not a supplement-brand study.
