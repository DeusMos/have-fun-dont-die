# FDA / Pfizer, 2013. Rapamune (sirolimus) prescribing information. U.S. Food and Drug Administration.
https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/021083s053,021110s067lbl.pdf
Used for: Transplant-dose daily sirolimus causes established, sometimes fatal, human infection and interstitial pneumonitis; that is not labeling theater, and it is not evidence that weekly geroscience doses are the same exposure.
Mark: ☠︎︎
N / effect / population / endpoint / duration: Boxed warning: increased susceptibility to infection (including opportunistic infection, tuberculosis, fatal infections, sepsis) and lymphoma/malignancy on immunosuppressive regimens. §5.11: interstitial lung disease including pneumonitis, BOOP, pulmonary fibrosis, some fatal, no identified infectious etiology; some resolved on dose cut or stop; risk rises with trough. Common (≥30%) AEs in transplant trials: peripheral edema, hypertriglyceridemia, hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, creatinine increase, abdominal pain, diarrhea, headache, fever, urinary tract infection, anemia, nausea, arthralgia, thrombocytopenia, pain. Hyperglycemia listed under metabolic. Exposure is daily transplant dosing with therapeutic troughs (loading up to 15 mg then 5 mg/d in some combo regimens) — not weekly 5–6 mg off-label geroscience practice. ☠︎︎ attaches to the transplant-dose harms as established; weekly autophagy-slogan use is a different exposure and is not hereby declared harmless or harmful.
