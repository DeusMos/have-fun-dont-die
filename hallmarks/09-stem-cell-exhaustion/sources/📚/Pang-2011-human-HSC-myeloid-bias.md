# Pang et al., 2011. Human bone marrow hematopoietic stem cells are increased in frequency and myeloid-biased with age. PNAS.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1116110108 PMID 22123971 PMC3250139
Used for: 📚 Aged human immunophenotypic HSCs are more frequent and less quiescent, yet myeloid-biased and worse at NSG engraftment — more marker-positive cells with worse function, not a missing-cell inventory.
Mark: 📚
N / effect / population / endpoint / duration: Healthy BM; young 20–35 y (flow n=13 HSC freq; culture n=8; xeno n=10 donors) vs elderly 65–85 y (flow n=11; culture n=6; xeno n=9). HSC as % of Lin−CD34+ higher in elderly (P<10−7). G0 fraction lower (P<0.013; n=13 young / 8 elderly). CLP frequency down (n=10/7; P<2.0×10−4). In vitro myeloid:lymphoid ratio up (P<0.011). Successful xeno engraftment: ~2-fold less human chimerism per 500 HSC (P<0.02 among engrafted; 6/10 young vs 6/9 elderly mice had any chimerism); myeloid:lymphoid ratio 14-fold higher (P<0.004). Total samples: 28 young, 15 elderly, 5 mid-age. Endpoint=flow frequency, AC6.2.1 culture, 16-week NSG chimerism. Cross-sectional.
