## Mechanism

📚 Raun et al. identified ipamorelin as a GHRP-like-receptor agonist that released GH from primary rat pituitary cells with EC50 1.3±0.4 nmol/L and Emax 85±5%, vs GHRP-6 2.2±0.3 nmol/L and 100%; GHRP and GHRH antagonists placed it on the GHRP-like receptor, not GHRH (Raun, Hansen, Johansen, Thøgersen, Madsen, Ankersen, and Andersen, *Eur. J. Endocrinol.*, 1998;139:552–561, PMID 9849822, https://doi.org/10.1530/eje.0.1390552).

## Pharmacokinetics / pharmacodynamics

📚 In pentobarbital-anaesthetised rats, ipamorelin GH ED50 was 80±42 nmol/kg (Emax 1545±250 ng/mL) vs GHRP-6 115±36 nmol/kg; in conscious swine, ED50 2.3±0.03 nmol/kg (Emax 65±0.2 ng/mL) vs GHRP-6 3.9±1.4 nmol/kg (Raun et al., 1998, PMID 9849822). Animal PD, not a human SubQ table.

## Animal data

📚 In swine, ipamorelin did not raise ACTH or cortisol above the GHRH-stimulation background even at doses >200-fold the GH ED50; GHRP-6 and GHRP-2 did raise ACTH/cortisol; none of the tested secretagogues moved FSH, LH, PRL, or TSH (Raun et al., 1998, PMID 9849822). Selectivity is a swine finding.

## Human data — aging, off-label, and geroscience

📚 Beck et al. (NCT00672074): multicenter double-blind RCT of IV ipamorelin 0.03 mg/kg twice daily vs placebo, postoperative day 1–7 or discharge, after small/large-bowel resection; 117 enrolled, 114 safety / modified ITT; median time to first tolerated solid meal 25.3 h vs 32.6 h (p=0.15); no significant differences on the key or secondary efficacy analyses; TEAEs 87.5% ipamorelin vs 94.8% placebo (Beck, Sweeney, and McCarter for the Ipamorelin 201 Study Group, *Int. J. Colorectal Dis.*, 2014;29:1527–1534, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00384-014-2030-8). Postoperative ileus, not aging, not a GH-axis RCT.

🐉 No published human aging / body-composition / GH-deficiency RCT of ipamorelin is compiled on this page. no source; inference from the empty aging file.

## Toxicity and hazards

📚 In Beck 2014 the overall TEAE rate was lower on ipamorelin than placebo (87.5% vs 94.8%) in a postoperative surgical population; that is not a chronic outpatient safety series (Beck et al., 2014, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00384-014-2030-8).
