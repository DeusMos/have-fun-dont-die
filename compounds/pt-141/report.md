## Identity

Home hallmark: [10 altered intercellular communication](../../hallmarks/10-altered-intercellular-communication/report.md) for the melanocortin / HSDD claim. Aliases: bremelanotide, Vyleesi, PT-141 free base. Adjacent cyclic analog: [melanotan-2](../melanotan-2/report.md). Adjacent linear analog: [melanotan-1](../melanotan-1/report.md).

💯 PT-141 is bremelanotide, a cyclic heptapeptide melanocortin-receptor agonist and the C-terminal carboxylic-acid analog of melanotan-II. It is not afamelanotide and it is not melanotan-II.

## Mechanism

📚 Vyleesi is labeled a melanocortin-receptor agonist. The indication is acquired, generalized HSDD in premenopausal women; it is not indicated for postmenopausal women, for men, or to enhance sexual performance (Vyleesi USPI, DailyMed).

## Pharmacokinetics / pharmacodynamics

📚 After 1.75 mg SC: mean Cmax 72.8 ng/mL, AUC 276 h·ng/mL, median Tmax ~1.0 h (0.5–1.0), absolute bioavailability ~100%, Vd 25.0 ± 5.8 L, t½ ~2.7 h (1.9–4.0), CL/F 6.5 ± 1.0 L/h, 21% plasma-protein bound. Exposure is less than dose-proportional from 0.3 to 10 mg SC; Cmax plateaus at 7.5 mg. Transient SBP +6 mmHg / DBP +3 mmHg peak 2–4 h, HR −5 bpm; usually back to baseline by 12 h (Vyleesi USPI, DailyMed).

## Human data — aging, off-label, and geroscience

📚 Two identical Phase 3 RCTs (NCT02333071, NCT02338960): premenopausal women with acquired generalized HSDD ≥6 months, 1.75 mg SC as needed vs placebo, 24-week core. Randomized 635 vs 632. Study 1 FSFI-Desire change: +0.5 (SD 1.1) vs +0.2 (1.0), median +0.6 vs 0, p=0.0002. Study 2: +0.6 (1.0) vs +0.2 (0.9), median +0.6 vs 0, p<0.0001. FSDS-DAO Q13: −0.7 vs −0.4 in both studies. Satisfying sexual events did not separate: Study 1 p=0.76, Study 2 p=0.70; mean change 0.0 on drug. Discontinuation 40% vs 13% (Study 1) and 39% vs 25% (Study 2) (Vyleesi USPI, DailyMed).

🐉 No published bremelanotide RCT with an aging or healthspan primary is compiled on this page. FSFI-Desire is not that endpoint. no source; inference from the empty aging file.

## Toxicity and hazards

📚 Nausea 40% vs 1% placebo; anti-emetic in 13%; 8% discontinued for nausea. Median onset ≤1 h, duration ~2 h; first-dose nausea 21%, later doses ~3%. Focal hyperpigmentation 1% at ≤8 doses/month; 38% after daily dosing for 8 days. Transient BP rise as above. Contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension or known cardiovascular disease. Not recommended at high CV risk or at >8 doses/month (Vyleesi USPI, DailyMed).

## Interactions

📚 May slow gastric emptying and change absorption of oral drugs. Significantly decreases systemic exposure of oral naltrexone; avoid oral naltrexone-containing products intended to treat alcohol or opioid addiction. Clinically relevant interaction also flagged for indomethacin (Vyleesi USPI, DailyMed).
