## Toxicity and hazards

☠︎︎ Daily beta-carotene 20 mg increased lung-cancer incidence 18% (95% CI 3–36%) and total mortality 8% (95% CI 1–16%) versus no beta-carotene in ATBC (ATBC Study Group, NEJM, 1994, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8127329/; N=29,133 male smokers aged 50–69, southwestern Finland; 5–8 years). Extracted from [01](../../hallmarks/01-genomic-instability/report.md).

☠︎︎ CARET was stopped 21 months early after a mean 4.0 years: beta-carotene 30 mg plus retinyl palmitate 25,000 IU daily raised lung-cancer RR to 1.28 (95% CI 1.04–1.57; P=0.02) and all-cause death RR to 1.17 (95% CI 1.03–1.33) versus placebo (Omenn et al., NEJM, 1996, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8602180/; N=18,314 smokers, former smokers, and asbestos-exposed workers; 388 lung cancers / 73,135 person-years). Extracted from [01](../../hallmarks/01-genomic-instability/report.md).

📚 Cochrane meta-analysis of antioxidant supplements found no mortality benefit; in 56 low-bias-risk trials antioxidants increased mortality (RR 1.04, 95% CI 1.01–1.07), driven by beta-carotene (RR 1.05) and vitamin E (RR 1.03) (Bjelakovic et al., Cochrane Database Syst Rev CD007176, 2012; N=78 trials / 296,707 participants in the full set). Extracted from [01](../../hallmarks/01-genomic-instability/report.md).

☠︎︎ The same ATBC 20 mg lung-cancer / mortality harm is reused in hallmark 04 against an antioxidant-as-protein-protection slogan (ATBC Study Group, NEJM, 1994, PMID 8127329). Extracted from [04](../../hallmarks/04-loss-of-proteostasis/report.md).

☠︎︎ The antioxidant-as-protection stack has a hard-endpoint record: ATBC high-dose beta-carotene raised lung cancer and death in smokers (ATBC, NEJM, 1994). That is not a documented body count for 20 mg MitoQ or 500 mg UA. Extracted from [07](../../hallmarks/07-mitochondrial-dysfunction/report.md).
