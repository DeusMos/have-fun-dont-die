## Mechanism

📚 The Wegovy label: semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist; it lowers calorie intake, likely via appetite, and lowers body weight with greater fat-mass than lean-mass loss (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 12.2).

📚 MASH mechanism in humans is not fully understood on that label and may involve weight-loss and other pathways; a diet-induced mouse MASH model showed histological steatosis / inflammation / fibrosis moves with weight loss (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 12.2). That is a labeled mechanism paragraph, not a healthspan result.

## Pharmacokinetics / pharmacodynamics

📚 Absolute bioavailability after subcutaneous Wegovy is 89%; Tmax 1 to 3 days; mean Vd ~12.5 L; plasma albumin binding >99%; apparent clearance ~0.05 L/h; elimination half-life approximately 1 week, so drug is present ~5 to 7 weeks after the last 2.4 mg or 7.2 mg injectable dose or 25 mg oral dose (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 12.3). That is labeled-product PK, not a gray-market vial table.

📚 Steady-state concentration after 2.4 mg once weekly SC is ~75 nmol/L in overweight/obesity; after 7.2 mg weekly ~230 nmol/L in obesity (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 12.3).

📚 Primary elimination is proteolytic cleavage of the peptide backbone plus sequential beta-oxidation of the fatty-acid side chain; ~3% of the dose appears in urine as intact semaglutide (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 12.3).

📚 Oral Wegovy tablets are co-formulated with SNAC; absolute bioavailability ~1–2%; Tmax ~1 hour; 25 mg daily is predicted comparable to 2.4 mg weekly injection with wider concentration spread (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 12.3).

📚 Semaglutide did not prolong QTc at SC doses up to 1.5 mg at steady state (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 12.2).

## Animal data

📚 In mice and rats, lifetime semaglutide produced dose- and duration-dependent thyroid C-cell adenomas and carcinomas at clinically relevant exposures; human relevance of that rodent finding is unknown on the label (DailyMed WEGOVY, boxed warning and section 13.1).

📚 Rat, rabbit, and cynomolgus studies during organogenesis showed embryofetal mortality, structural abnormalities, and growth effects at clinically relevant exposures, coinciding with marked maternal weight loss in rabbit and monkey (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 8.1). Label developmental tox, not an aging lifespan study.

🐉 No compiled semaglutide rodent lifespan or healthy-aging dataset was fetched this round (inference from the labeled-product file).

## Human data — aging, off-label, and geroscience

📚 SELECT: weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg cut MACE in people with obesity and established CVD (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72–0.90; 6.5% vs 8.0%; n=17,604; mean 39.8 months) (Lincoff et al., NEJM, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563, PMID 37952131). Novo Nordisk funded. Indicated secondary prevention, not hallmark-06 restoration in metabolically healthy buyers. Extracted from [06](../../hallmarks/06-deregulated-nutrient-sensing/report.md#human-data).

📚 DailyMed Study 2 (NCT03548935; STEP-1 population): 1,961 adults with obesity or overweight plus a comorbidity, no type 2 diabetes, 2:1 to Wegovy 2.4 mg weekly vs placebo × 68 weeks; mean baseline weight 105.3 kg, BMI 37.9; % change −14.9 vs −2.4 (difference −12.4, 95% CI −13.3 to −11.6); ≥5% loss 83.5% vs 31.1% (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 14.2, Table 8). Obesity weight-loss trial, not healthspan.

📚 DailyMed Study 3 (NCT03552757): 807 adults with type 2 diabetes and BMI ≥27; −9.6% vs −3.4% at 68 weeks (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 14.2, Table 8).

📚 DailyMed Study 4 (NCT03611582): intensive-lifestyle background; −16% vs −5.7% at 68 weeks (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 14.2, Table 8).

📚 Wegovy injection is indicated, with diet and activity, to cut MACE in adults with established CVD plus obesity or overweight; to reduce and maintain weight in adults and pediatric patients ≥12 with obesity, and in adults with overweight plus a weight-related comorbidity; and, under accelerated approval, for noncirrhotic MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 1). Those are labeled disease indications.

🐉 No compiled semaglutide RCT with a healthspan, mortality-in-healthy-adults, or epigenetic-age primary endpoint was found this round (Lincoff 2023; DailyMed WEGOVY).

## Toxicity and hazards

📚 Boxed warning: rodent C-cell tumors; contraindicated in personal or family MTC or MEN 2; also contraindicated in known hypersensitivity to semaglutide or Wegovy excipients (DailyMed WEGOVY, boxed warning and section 4).

📚 Warnings compiled on the label: acute pancreatitis (including fatal/nonfatal hemorrhagic or necrotizing); acute gallbladder disease; hypoglycemia with insulin or an insulin secretagogue; AKI from volume depletion after GI losses; severe GI reactions / not recommended in severe gastroparesis; hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis and angioedema; diabetic-retinopathy complications (SUSTAIN-6-class 3% vs 1.8% at 0.5/1 mg in T2D plus high CV risk; larger absolute rise if prior retinopathy); heart-rate increase 1–4 bpm mean, with 20 bpm peaks in 26% vs 16%; pulmonary aspiration under general anesthesia or deep sedation (DailyMed WEGOVY, sections 5.2–5.10).

📚 Weight-reduction trials: GI AEs 73% vs 47% placebo; nausea 44% vs 16%, vomiting 25% vs 6%, diarrhea 30% vs 16%; permanent discontinuation 6.8% vs 3.2%, mostly nausea / vomiting / diarrhea (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 6.1).

📚 SELECT safety collection was limited; 16% vs 8% discontinued for an AE over median ~37–39 months (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 6.1).

📚 Appendicitis including perforated: 0.5% vs 0.2% in adult weight-reduction trials (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 6.1).

📚 Overdose reports for the class include severe nausea, vomiting, and hypoglycemia; observation may need to cover the ~1-week half-life (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 10).

🐉 No published case series establishing sure, meaningful harm from gray-market SM-GLP1 in ordinary adults was compiled this round. Label GI / pancreatitis / gallbladder / retinopathy / C-cell boxed text are labeled-product risks, not ☠︎︎ for the vial. Stay-away is not issued.

## Interactions

📚 Semaglutide is not expected to inhibit or induce CYP enzymes or drug transporters at clinically relevant concentrations on the label (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 12.3).

📚 Delay of gastric emptying can change absorption of oral drugs; at 1 mg weekly steady state, clinical-pharmacology studies did not show a clinically relevant PK hit on the tested oral medications; the oral tablet raised levothyroxine exposure 33% (90% CI 1.25–1.42) (DailyMed WEGOVY, sections 7.2 and 12.3).

📚 Concomitant insulin or insulin secretagogue (e.g. sulfonylurea) raises hypoglycemia risk; the label says consider reducing those doses when starting Wegovy (DailyMed WEGOVY, sections 5.4 and 7.1).

📚 Concomitant use with other semaglutide products or any other GLP-1 receptor agonist is not recommended (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 1, Limitations of Use).

📚 For CV-risk or weight indications, discontinue at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy because of the long half-life (DailyMed WEGOVY, section 8.3).
