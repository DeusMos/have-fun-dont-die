## Identity

Home hallmark: [03 epigenetic alterations](../../hallmarks/03-epigenetic-alterations/report.md). Adjacent: [methylcobalamin](../methylcobalamin/report.md). Aliases: vitamin B12 (as cyano), CN-Cbl. This page is the cyanide-cobalt synthetic vitamer, not methylcobalamin.

💯 Cyanocobalamin is the CN–Co form of cobalamin; it is converted in vivo to MeCbl and AdoCbl.

## Mechanism

💯 After decyanation, the cobalt center is rebuilt as MeCbl or AdoCbl. The cyanide ligand is not the active cofactor.

📚 USP injection labels describe cyanocobalamin as the stable synthetic B12 used for IM/SC replacement and the Schilling test (DailyMed Cyanocobalamin Injection USP, https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a61181f5-7e2d-45f9-929a-cb8bc70c992e).

## Human data — aging, off-label, and geroscience

📚 Labeled indications: vitamin B12 deficiency including pernicious anemia; Schilling test. Parenteral B12 is lifelong in pernicious anemia (DailyMed cyanocobalamin injection).

📚 Sawangjit 2020 is a mecobalamin neuropathy meta, not a cyanocobalamin-vs-placebo aging RCT (Sawangjit et al., *J. Altern. Complement. Med.*, 2020;26:1117–1129, https://doi.org/10.1089/acm.2020.0068).

⛔ Repleting a deficient patient is not a human aging RCT.

🐉 No compiled healthy-aging or lifespan trial of cyanocobalamin is on this page. no source; inference from the empty aging-RCT file.

## Toxicity and hazards

📚 Cyanocobalamin injection label: hypokalemia and sudden death may occur when severe megaloblastic anemia is treated intensely; watch serum potassium the first 48 hours in pernicious anemia and replace if needed. Anaphylaxis reported; some labels recommend a dermal test dose (DailyMed cyanocobalamin injection).

⛔ The cyanide-ligand slogan as “toxic B12” for physiologic microgram doses is not a ☠︎︎ finding. The labeled harm is hypokalemia during intense repletion of severe megaloblastic anemia, not bleach-class cyanide poisoning.

## Interactions

📚 The insert: folic acid can mask B12 hematologic failure while neuropathy progresses; give B12, not folate alone, when both are missing (DailyMed cyanocobalamin injection).
