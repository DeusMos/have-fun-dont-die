## Identity

Home hallmark: [07 mitochondrial dysfunction](../../hallmarks/07-mitochondrial-dysfunction/report.md). Aliases: MOTS-c, mitochondrial open reading frame of the 12S rRNA-c, MDP. Related stack SKUs already split out: [ss-31](../ss-31/report.md), [nad](../nad/report.md). Humanin and SHLP1–6 sit in the same mitochondrial-derived-peptide reviews; they do not have dirs here.

📚 PubChem CID 146675088 lists MOTS-c as sequence MRWQEMGYIFYPRKLR, CAS 1627580-64-6 (ChemIDplus), formula C101H152N28O22S2, MW 2174.6 g/mol, UNII A5CV6JFB78; a depositor synonym on that CID is “MOTS-c (human) (trifluoroacetate salt)” (PubChem, https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/146675088; modify date 2026-08-31).

📚 FDA SRS preferred name for UNII A5CV6JFB78 is MITOCHONDRIA-DERIVED PEPTIDE MOTS-C and maps the same CAS and sequence; UNII availability does not imply review or approval (FDA SRS, https://precision.fda.gov/uniisearch/srs/unii/a5cv6jfb78).

📚 Lee et al. reported a 51-bp sORF in mitochondrial 12S rRNA that encodes that 16-aa peptide; mitochondrial translation would yield tandem start/stop, so the viable peptide is produced by the cytoplasmic code (Lee et al., Cell Metabolism, 2015, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2015.02.009, PMID 25738459, PMC4350682).

📚 FDA staff treated MOTS-c free base and MOTS-c acetate as distinct 503A bulk-drug substances and said a free base versus a salt can have different safety or efficacy profiles; commercial naming of salts under one common name was already inconsistent in that file (FDA, MOTS-c BDS briefing, 2026-05-11, https://www.fda.gov/media/193347/download).

🤔 Mindful Research lists the same CAS and CID 146675088 on the shop SKU (https://happypeptides.com/products/mots-c; catalog pull September 3, 2026). Storefront CAS is a shop claim.

## The claim and the slogan

🤔 Longevity and peptide-clinic copy sells injected MOTS-c as exercise-in-a-syringe / mitochondrial restore / metabolic youth: Telehealth Ally and PeptideClarity traffic 5–10 mg SubQ; Defy Medical lists MOTS-C for cellular energy and insulin-sensitivity copy; Mindful Research sells 10 mg and 40 mg RUO vials (Telehealth Ally, https://telehealthally.com/peptides/mots-c; PeptideClarity, https://getpeptideclarity.com/compounds/mots-c; Defy Medical, https://www.defymedical.com/blog/what-are-peptides-and-how-do-they-work/; Mindful Research, https://happypeptides.com/products/mots-c). Those pages sell or refer the peptide.

⛔ Treating Reynolds 2021 endogenous exercise physiology or mouse IP 5–15 mg/kg as proof that a 5–10 mg human SubQ vial restores mitochondria or metabolic youth is a definition swap: different operation, different exposure, different species, different endpoint (Reynolds et al., Nature Communications, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20790-0; Telehealth Ally, https://telehealthally.com/peptides/mots-c).

⛔ A research-chemical CAS/HPLC listing is not a human aging trial and not a completed restore result (Mindful Research terms, https://happypeptides.com/policies/terms-of-service).

⛔ A PCAC advisory recommendation is compounding-list policy, not a demonstration that injected MOTS-c restores mitochondria or treats human aging (FDA, https://www.fda.gov/media/193347/download; McDermott Will & Emery, 2026, https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/bulk-list-bound-pcac-backs-majority-of-peptides-in-two-day-public-meeting/).

## Mechanism

📚 In HEK293, MOTS-c overexpression or 10 μM peptide inhibited the folate-methionine cycle and tethered de novo purine biosynthesis, raised endogenous AICAR more than 20-fold in MOTS-c-ST cells, and phosphorylated AMPKα (Thr172) in a time- and dose-dependent manner (Lee et al., Cell Metabolism, 2015, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2015.02.009; N=5 metabolomics / 6 microarray; 4–72 h).

📚 Metabolic stress (glucose restriction 0.5 g/L, serum deprivation, or 100 μM tBHP) drove MOTS-c into the HEK293 nucleus by 30 min in an AMPK-dependent manner; wild-type overexpression protected cells from 96 h glucose/serum restriction while nuclear-loss-of-function mutants (8YIFY11→AAAA, 13RKLR16→AAAA) did not (Kim et al., Cell Metabolism, 2018, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.06.008, PMID 29983246; n=3 imaging, n=6 RNA-seq).

📚 Raising MOTS-c concentration alone did not trigger nuclear translocation in that system (Kim et al., Cell Metabolism, 2018, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.06.008).

📚 In human whole blood at 37°C, MOTS-c 5 µg/mL underwent rapid N-terminal proteolytic hydrolysis to MOTS-c(2–16) through (5–16) within 15–60 minutes (Knoop, Thomas, Thevis, Rapid Communications in Mass Spectrometry, 2019, https://doi.org/10.1002/rcm.8337, PMID 30408314).

📚 Miller et al. review eight published MDPs — humanin, MOTS-c, SHLP1–6 — with MOTS-c from 12S rRNA and humanin/SHLPs from 16S rRNA (Miller et al., JCI, 2022, https://www.jci.org/articles/view/158449).

🐉 Collapsing MOTS-c + humanin + SHLPs into one injectable “MDP youth” story is a class-review leap, not a shared human aging result (inference from Miller 2022 + ADDF briefs; no shared aging RCT).

## Pharmacokinetics / pharmacodynamics

🐉 No published t½ / Vd / CL / F table for exogenous native MOTS-c was compiled this round; ADDF prints Half-life: N/A (ADDF MOTS-c brief, 2025, https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/MOTS-c.pdf). Do not invent one.

📚 NCT03998514 lists apparent terminal t½ of CB4211 — an analogue, not native MOTS-c — as a secondary endpoint; results are not posted (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT03998514, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03998514; hasResults=false).

⛔ Telehealth Ally’s claim that a first-in-human PK study confirmed safety and bioavailability of subcutaneous MOTS-c does not match a posted native-MOTS-c PK paper (Telehealth Ally, https://telehealthally.com/peptides/mots-c; NCT03998514; ADDF brief).

📚 The Knoop blood-hydrolysis time course is the compiled exogenous-stability fact, not a human SubQ PK curve (Knoop et al., Rapid Communications in Mass Spectrometry, 2019, https://doi.org/10.1002/rcm.8337).

## Animal data

📚 Daily IP MOTS-c improved mouse insulin action and blocked diet-induced obesity: 5 mg/kg/day × 7 days improved IPGTT in male C57BL/6 (n=7) and raised clamp glucose-infusion rate ~30% on 60% HFD (n=6–8); 5 mg/kg/day × 7 days restored 12-month soleus insulin-stimulated 2-deoxyglucose uptake toward 3-month levels (n=6); 0.5 mg/kg/day IP × 8 weeks prevented HFD obesity in male CD-1 mice (n=10) without changing caloric intake (Lee et al., Cell Metabolism, 2015, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2015.02.009). Lee was a CohBar consultant; Cohen was a CohBar stockholder/consultant.

📚 Daily IP MOTS-c raised treadmill capacity in mice: young CD-1 males at 15 mg/kg/day ran longer and farther than 0 or 5 mg/kg after 10 days (n=5 control/5 mg/kg, n=6 at 15 mg/kg); young C57BL/6J at 15 mg/kg/day × 2 weeks ran longer on chow and 60% HFD (n=8); 22-month C57BL/6N males at 15 mg/kg/day × 2 weeks ran ~2-fold longer and 2.16-fold farther (n=19 old control, n=18 old MOTS-c) (Reynolds et al., Nature Communications, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20790-0).

📚 Late-life intermittent IP MOTS-c (15 mg/kg, 3×/week from 23.5 months) improved late-life physical tests in male C57BL/6N mice (grip n=11, p=0.000078; stride n=5, p=0.0038; 60-s walk n=11/12, p=0.0428); the full lifespan curve trended +6.4% median / +7.0% maximum and was not significant overall (p=0.23), with a truncated analysis to 31.8 months reported at p=0.05 (Reynolds et al., Nature Communications, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20790-0).

📚 Those Reynolds and Lee results are mouse IP pharmacology — capacity, HFD obesity, clamp insulin action — not a human SubQ aging trial and not a demonstration that injecting the peptide restores mitochondria (Lee et al., 2015; Reynolds et al., 2021).

📚 Daily IP MOTS-c 5 mg/kg for 12 weeks after ovariectomy reduced micro-CT bone loss in mice and inhibited RANKL-driven osteoclast differentiation via AMPK; N was not stated in the abstract retrieved this round (Ming et al., BBRC, 2016, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2016.05.135).

📚 In male Sprague–Dawley rats (n=8/group), 0.5 mg/kg/day IP MOTS-c for 12 weeks plus treadmill training raised echocardiographic EF versus sedentary control (p<0.001); several P–V indices (stroke work, cardiac output, Powmax) were lower in MOTS-c+exercise than exercise alone (Yuan et al., Scientific Reports, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-99568-3).

📚 Weng et al. 2019 claiming MOTS-c accelerates bone-fracture healing via FOXF1/TGF-β in rat BMSCs was withdrawn by the authors in 2021 for image-organization inaccuracies (Weng et al., Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci, 2019, PMID 31858528; withdrawal 2021;25:2459, https://doi.org/10.26355/eurrev_202103_25396).

🤔 ADDF states peripherally administered MOTS-c had no effect on cognition in mice at a capacity-improving dose (ADDF MOTS-c brief, https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/MOTS-c.pdf).

## Human data — aging, off-label, and geroscience

📚 In 10 sedentary healthy young men (24.5 ± 3.7 y, BMI 24.1 ± 2.1), a bicycle bout raised endogenous skeletal-muscle MOTS-c 11.9-fold after exercise (Wilcoxon p=0.0098) and circulating MOTS-c 1.6-fold during / 1.5-fold after exercise (p=0.0011, 0.0021), returning toward baseline after 4 h rest; no exogenous peptide was injected (Reynolds et al., Nature Communications, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20790-0).

🐉 No completed native-MOTS-c anti-aging or healthspan RCT was on ClinicalTrials.gov this round; a 2026-09-03 `query.term=MOTS-c` pull returned one interventional native-MOTS-c drug trial plus analogue and biomarker studies (ClinicalTrials.gov API, https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.term=MOTS-c&pageSize=20).

📚 NCT07505745 (MOTS-MET) is a recruiting Phase 2a RCT of investigational MOTS-c (MDP) versus placebo, once-daily SubQ for 12 weeks, in adults 18–65 with prediabetes and BMI 27–40; estimated n=120; primary endpoints are 12-week change in OGTT Matsuda index and 16-week TEAEs; sponsor Hudson Biotech; site Peking University Shenzhen Hospital; start 2026-02-02; hasResults=false; mg dose is not posted (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT07505745, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745).

🐉 NCT07505745 is insulin-sensitivity in prediabetes, not aging, not mitochondrial restore, and not an identity-match to research-chem vials (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT07505745).

📚 NCT03998514 is a completed Phase 1a/1b of CB4211 — a MOTS-c analogue, not native MOTS-c — in healthy non-obese adults and subjects with NAFLD; actual enrollment 88; primary outcomes were safety, labs, ECG, and injection-site assessments; completed 2021-04-19; hasResults=false (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT03998514, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03998514; sponsor CohBar, Inc.).

🤔 In the 4-week inpatient Phase 1b NAFLD cohort, CB4211 25 mg SubQ daily (n=11) versus placebo (n=9) reduced ALT −21% vs +4% and AST −28% vs −11% (p<0.05 vs placebo) while MRI-PDFF liver-fat change was −5.03% vs −4.88% and >30% relative-fat responders were 36% vs 33%; both arms were confined and fed a standardized diet (Loomba, Hompesch, Salazar, Lawitz, Kam, Cundy, AASLD 2021 poster LB5, CohBar-sponsored, https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_fd6f24196aada5de22257a4c9e2a05ff/cohbar/db/481/4000/pdf/AASLD+2021+Poster+LB5+Final+for+Submission+25Oct2021.pdf).

🤔 CohBar stated in its 2023 S-4 that the CB4211 Phase 1b formulation was not suitable for further development; ADDF states clinical development of the analogue was discontinued after CohBar dissolved (CohBar Form S-4/A, 2023, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1522602/000121390023065729/fs42023a1_cohbar.htm; ADDF MOTS-c brief, https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/MOTS-c.pdf).

📚 Circulating MOTS-c by Cloud-Clone ELISA did not change after 24 weeks of neoadjuvant chemo/trastuzumab with or without metformin 850 mg BID in 38 HER2+ breast-cancer patients from METTEN, and did not differ by pathological complete response (Cuyàs, Verdura, Martín-Castillo, Menéndez, Aging, 2022, https://doi.org/10.18632/aging.204426, PMID 36490309, PMC10008497; n=19/arm).

📚 A meta-analysis of 6 case-control studies and 1 cross-sectional study (11 groups, 602 participants) found circulating MOTS-c lower in T2DM (SMD −0.89, 95% CI −1.12 to −0.65) but higher in obesity after excluding overweight (SMD +0.51, 95% CI 0.21 to 0.81) (Zhou et al., Diabetology & Metabolic Syndrome, 2024, https://doi.org/10.1186/s13098-024-01405-w, PMID 39160573).

📚 Circulating MOTS-c was lower in obese versus control children/adolescents (472.61 ± 22.83 vs 561.64 ± 19.19 ng/mL, P<0.01), driven by males (Du et al., Pediatric Diabetes, 2018, https://doi.org/10.1111/pedi.12685, PMID 29707908).

📚 Circulating MOTS-c was higher in obese adults scheduled for bariatric surgery than in lean controls (273 ± 56 vs 223 ± 50 pg/mL, P<0.01; n=32 vs 22) and was unchanged 6 months after surgery despite BMI drop (P=0.913; n=10) (Yoon, Lerman et al., Journal of Clinical and Translational Endocrinology, 2025, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcte.2025.100429, PMID 41551324).

⛔ Observational “low circulating MOTS-c in disease” is not an inject-to-restore aging result (Yoon et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2024).

## Toxicity and hazards

📚 FDA staff’s 2026 PCAC evaluation concluded the 503A criteria weigh against listing MOTS-c free base or acetate: no USP/NF monograph, neither is a component of an FDA-approved drug, free base is not well-characterized, FDA identified no human exposure data via any route, potential human safety risks are unknown, and there is a lack of evidence to evaluate effectiveness for the nominated uses, including longevity (FDA, MOTS-c BDS briefing, 2026-05-11, https://www.fda.gov/media/193347/download; nominated SC 5 mg and 10 mg).

📚 FDA’s withdrawn Category 2 nomination text states compounded MOTS-c may pose significant immunogenicity risk for certain routes, peptide-related impurities and API characterization are complex, and FDA lacks information on whether MOTS-c would cause harm if administered to humans (FDA, Certain Bulk Drug Substances… Significant Safety Risks, https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks).

📚 No FAERS reports for MOTS-c were retrieved by FDA’s Office of Surveillance and Epidemiology through 2025-03-09; empty FAERS is absence of coded reports, not a safety trial (FDA MOTS-c briefing, https://www.fda.gov/media/193347/download).

🤔 ADDF states native MOTS-c has not been clinically tested as a therapeutic; CB4211 short-term Phase 1 was described as safe/well tolerated with common persistent injection-site reactions (painless bumps) that paused the 2018 protocol (ADDF MOTS-c brief, https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/MOTS-c.pdf).

🤔 USADA compiles self-experimenter reports of tachycardia/palpitations, injection-site irritation, insomnia, immune reactions, and fever — anecdote, not a controlled harm dataset (USADA, https://www.usada.org/spirit-of-sport/what-is-mots-c-peptide/).

🐉 No established human harm from MOTS-c itself — as opposed to class immunogenicity concern, analogue injection-site reactions, or gray-market anecdote — was found this round; that gap is not ☠︎︎ and is not proof of safety (FDA MOTS-c briefing; USADA; Loomba et al., AASLD 2021 LB5).

## Interactions

🤔 ADDF states MOTS-c may interact with other AMPK-targeting drugs, primarily antidiabetic agents such as metformin; that is mechanism overlap, not a labeled interaction (ADDF MOTS-c brief, https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/MOTS-c.pdf).

🤔 PeptideClarity flags insulin/sulfonylurea hypoglycemia as a practice warning; Telehealth Ally lists mild injection-site reactions and “active malignancy (theoretical)” (PeptideClarity, https://getpeptideclarity.com/compounds/mots-c; Telehealth Ally, https://telehealthally.com/peptides/mots-c). Those pages traffic the peptide.

📚 NCT07505745 excludes active malignancy requiring treatment, pregnancy, eGFR <60, and recent glucose-lowering drugs — protocol exclusions, not a labeled safety monograph (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT07505745).

🐉 There is no FDA-labeled MOTS-c product and no compiled CYP/transporter interaction table was found this round (ADDF brief; NCT07505745 hasResults false).

## Fights

🥼 Circulating “MOTS-c” is not one number: Reynolds reports ~125–190 pg/mL around a bike test; commercial ELISAs report tens to hundreds of ng/mL; Knoop’s WADA-validated LC/MS (LLOD 100 pg/mL) did not confirm ELISA values of 45.9–218.5 ng/mL in a 20-person set (Reynolds et al., Nature Communications, 2021; Knoop et al., RCM, 2019, https://doi.org/10.1002/rcm.8337; Cuyàs et al., Aging, 2022).

🥼 Whether circulating MOTS-c is low or high in obesity is a live observational fight (lower in obese male children, Du 2018; higher in obese adults and unchanged after weight loss, Yoon 2025; meta-analysis opposite directions for T2DM vs obesity, Zhou 2024); none of those papers inject MOTS-c (Du et al., Pediatric Diabetes, 2018; Yoon et al., JCTE, 2025; Zhou et al., 2024).

🤼 Injected MOTS-c as a longevity protocol versus Reynolds 2021 endogenous exercise MOTS-c is an amateur/clinic split (Reynolds et al., 2021; Telehealth Ally; PeptideClarity).

🤼 5 vs 10 mg, 2 vs 3×/week, 8–12 week cycle versus continuous (and daily-10 mg copy on some protocol blogs) is split across Telehealth Ally, PeptideClarity, and peptidedosingprotocols; no published human RCT compares those schedules (same URLs).

🤼 Telehealth Ally (“Not WADA-Listed”) and PeptideClarity May 2026 (“not currently on WADA’s prohibited list”) contradict the named 2026 S4 entry and Peptide Dosing Protocols’ “WADA bans it at all times” line (Telehealth Ally; PeptideClarity; peptidedosingprotocols.com; WADA 2026 Prohibited List, https://www.wada-ama.org/sites/default/files/2025-09/2026list_en_final_clean_september_2025.pdf).

## Open questions

## What is actually on the table

📚 MOTS-c is a real 16-aa mtDNA-encoded peptide with a Lee 2015 folate–AICAR–AMPK file and Reynolds 2021 mouse capacity plus n=10 endogenous exercise fold-change (Lee et al., Cell Metabolism, 2015; Reynolds et al., Nature Communications, 2021).

⛔ That biology is not a completed human restore result and not a license to read a RUO or compounded vial as exercise-in-a-syringe (Reynolds et al., 2021; Mindful Research terms; FDA briefing).

🐉 The one registered native-MOTS-c interventional trial is recruiting for insulin sensitivity, not aging, and has no results (NCT07505745).

🤔 People can buy 10/40 mg RUO vials and 5–10 mg compounded SubQ protocols today. That is practice, not healthspan (Mindful Research; Telehealth Ally; PeptideClarity).
