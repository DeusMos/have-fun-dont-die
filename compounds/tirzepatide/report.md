## Identity

Home hallmark: [06 deregulated nutrient sensing](../../hallmarks/06-deregulated-nutrient-sensing/report.md). Aliases: Mounjaro, Zepbound, LY3298176, T-GLP2, GLP-1/GIP. Adjacent incretin SKUs: [semaglutide](../semaglutide/report.md), [retatrutide](../retatrutide/report.md), [cagrilintide](../cagrilintide/report.md).

💯 Tirzepatide is a glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) receptor and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, setid 487cd7e7-434c-4925-99fa-aa80b1cc776b).

📚 PubChem CID 156588324 is titled Tirzepatide, formula C225H348N48O68, MW 4813, RN 2023788-19-2 (PubChem PUG CID/RN fetched 2026-09-04, https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/156588324).

📚 NCATS GSRS names CAS 2023788-19-2 as primary and UNII OYN3CCI6QE (https://gsrs.ncats.nih.gov/ginas/app/ui/substances/55b0b673-89ca-4436-bb84-d46a166457dc).

📚 The Zepbound label: molecular weight 4813.53 Da, empirical formula C225H348N48O68; GIP-sequence peptide with Aib at positions 2 and 13, C-terminal amide, and Lys-20 linked to 1,20-eicosanedioic acid (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 11).

## Mechanism

📚 The Zepbound label: tirzepatide selectively binds and activates GIP and GLP-1 receptors; a C20 fatty diacid enables albumin binding and prolongs half-life; it stimulates insulin secretion glucose-dependently, reduces glucagon, and increased insulin sensitivity in a 28-week clamp in type 2 diabetes; it delays gastric emptying, largest after the first dose and diminishing later (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, sections 12.1–12.2).

## Pharmacokinetics / pharmacodynamics

📚 Elimination half-life is approximately 5–6 days in overweight/obesity and in OSA plus obesity; apparent clearance ~0.06 L/h (CV ~20%); overdose observation should cover ~5 days (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, sections 10 and 12.3). That is labeled-product PK, not a gray-market vial table.

📚 Gastric-emptying delay is greatest after a single 5 mg dose and diminishes after subsequent doses (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 12.3).

## Animal data

📚 In a 2-year rat study, tirzepatide produced dose- and duration-dependent thyroid C-cell adenomas and carcinomas at clinically relevant exposures (0.1–1× the 15 mg weekly MRHD by AUC); a 6-month rasH2 mouse study was not tumorigenic (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 13.1). Human relevance of the rodent C-cell finding is unknown on the label.

🐉 No compiled tirzepatide rodent lifespan or healthy-aging dataset was fetched this round (inference from the labeled-product file).

## Human data — aging, off-label, and geroscience

📚 DailyMed Study 1 (NCT04184622; SURMOUNT-1 population): 2,539 adults with obesity or overweight plus a comorbidity, no type 2 diabetes, 1:1:1:1 to Zepbound 5 / 10 / 15 mg weekly vs placebo × 72 weeks; mean baseline weight 104.8 kg, BMI 38; % change −15.0 / −19.5 / −20.9 vs −3.1; ≥5% loss 85.1 / 88.9 / 90.9% vs 34.5% (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 14.1, Table 2). Obesity weight-loss trial, not healthspan.

📚 Zepbound is indicated, with diet and activity, to reduce and maintain weight in adults with obesity or overweight plus a weight-related comorbidity, and to treat moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 1). ## Toxicity and hazards

📚 Boxed warning: rodent C-cell tumors; contraindicated in personal or family MTC or MEN 2; also contraindicated in known serious hypersensitivity to tirzepatide or Zepbound excipients (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, boxed warning and section 4).

📚 Warnings compiled on the label: severe GI reactions / not recommended in severe gastroparesis; AKI from volume depletion; acute gallbladder disease; acute pancreatitis; hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis and angioedema; hypoglycemia with insulin or an insulin secretagogue; diabetic-retinopathy complications in type 2 diabetes (added 08/2026); pulmonary aspiration under general anesthesia or deep sedation (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 5).

📚 Pooled Study 1+2 weight-reduction trials (n=2,519 on drug up to 72 weeks): permanent discontinuation for AE 4.8 / 6.3 / 6.7% at 5 / 10 / 15 mg vs 3.4% placebo, mostly GI in the first months. Nausea 25 / 29 / 28% vs 8%; diarrhea 19 / 21 / 23% vs 8%; vomiting 8 / 11 / 13% vs 2%; GI events overall 56% vs 30% (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 6.1, Table 1).

📚 Other compiled label events in that pool: hypotension 1.6% vs 0.1%; immediate hypersensitivity 2.1% vs 0.4%; hair loss 7.1% of women vs 0.5% of men on drug; AKI 0.5% vs 0.2%; cholecystitis 0.7% vs 0.2%; mean heart-rate +1–3 bpm (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 6.1).

📚 In Study 2 (T2D + BMI ≥27), plasma glucose <54 mg/dL occurred in 4.2% vs 1.3%; with a sulfonylurea 10.3% vs 2.1% without (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 5.7).

🐉 No published case series establishing sure, meaningful harm from gray-market T-GLP2 in ordinary adults was compiled this round. Label GI / pancreatitis / gallbladder / C-cell boxed text are labeled-product risks, not ☠︎︎ for the vial. Stay-away is not issued.

## Interactions

📚 Delay of gastric emptying can change absorption of oral drugs; the delay is largest after the first dose and shrinks later (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, sections 7.2 and 12.2).

📚 Oral hormonal contraceptives may lose efficacy during that delay; the label tells patients using oral contraceptives to switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier for 4 weeks after start and after each escalation (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, sections 7.2 and 8.3).

📚 Concomitant insulin or insulin secretagogue raises hypoglycemia risk; the label says consider reducing those doses when starting Zepbound (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, sections 5.7 and 7.1).

📚 Coadministration with other tirzepatide products or any GLP-1 receptor agonist is not recommended (DailyMed ZEPBOUND, section 1, Limitations of Use).
