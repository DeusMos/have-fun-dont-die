# Topic: Salamander-like regeneration

**Evidence cutoff:** September 3, 2026
**Last updated:** September 3, 2026

This repository is not medical advice. It is a sourced information dump for anyone who wants the details.

🤔 A 2026 press cycle sold a Wake Forest / Duke / Morgridge–UW paper as a “holy grail salamander gene” that lets mice regrow digits and points at replacing human limbs with living tissue instead of prosthetics (Wake Forest News, 16 Apr 2026, https://news.wfu.edu/2026/04/16/for-regrowing-human-limbs-this-salamander-gene-could-hold-the-key/; ScienceDaily, 9 May 2026, https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260508003121.htm).

📚 The manuscript those headlines sit on is Brown, Currie, Poss et al.: conserved epidermal SP6/SP8 factors in zebrafish fin, axolotl limb, and mouse digit-tip regenerates, plus an injury-gated AAV that delivers FGF8 under a zebrafish tissue-regeneration enhancer and changes mouse P3 bone on micro-CT (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2532804123, PMID 41980086; numbers below from the accepted Dec 2025 bioRxiv full text of the same manuscript, https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.01.691633v1).

⛔ That is not a salamander-only gene copied into mice, not whole-limb epimorphosis in a mammal, and not a clinic product (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; Wake Forest News, 2026; ScienceDaily, 2026).

## The claim and the slogan

The scientific claim under test is a conserved epidermal SP6/SP8 → FGF8 module across zebrafish caudal fin, axolotl mid-zeugopod limb, and mouse P3 digit tip, plus an AAV.cc47 vector that expresses FGF8 from the zebrafish leptin-b–linked enhancer (LEN) and can partially rescue SP-deficient mouse P3 bone or accelerate wild-type P3 regrowth.

The slogan in the wild is that labs found the salamander regeneration gene and used gene therapy so mice — and soon people — grow limbs back.

📚 Brown et al. titled the paper as enhancer-directed gene delivery for *digit* regeneration and called instructing regeneration of complex structures in mammals an unsolved problem (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2532804123).

🤔 Wake Forest News framed SP genes as a “salamander gene” that “could hold the key” to “regrowing human limbs,” while the body text names digit-tip gene therapy and FGF8 via a zebrafish enhancer (Roberts / Wake Forest University, 16 Apr 2026, https://news.wfu.edu/2026/04/16/for-regrowing-human-limbs-this-salamander-gene-could-hold-the-key/).

🤔 ScienceDaily ran the Wake Forest materials as a “holy grail” gene that could help humans regrow limbs and “a major step toward future treatments that could replace damaged limbs with living tissue instead of prosthetics” (ScienceDaily / Wake Forest University, 9 May 2026, https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260508003121.htm).

🤔 Fight Aging restated the PNAS abstract as enhancer-guided FGF8 that “enhanced the ability of mice to regenerate lost digit tips” and called that “a modest starting point,” not whole-limb salamander transfer (Reason / Fight Aging, Apr 2026, https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/04/carefully-guided-fgf8-expression-via-gene-therapy-enhances-digit-tip-regrowth-in-mice/).

🤼 University/ScienceDaily “holy grail / replace prosthetics with living limbs” copy and Fight Aging’s “modest digit-tip starting point” are the same 2026 paper sold at two slogan temperatures (ScienceDaily, 2026; Fight Aging, 2026).

⛔ Calling SP6/SP8 a transplanted “salamander gene” product fails the paper: those are conserved C2H2 zinc-finger transcription factors already in mouse and human; the AAV cargo is mouse Fgf8 driven by zebrafish LEN, not Amex.Sp8 (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; Wake Forest News, 2026; ScienceDaily, 2026).

📚 Aravind Asokan is a co-founder and director at Torque Bio and is listed as an inventor on patents covering subject matter in the 2026 manuscript (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026, acknowledgments).

📚 Nearby true facts that get stacked into the slogan include endogenous mouse P3 competence, axolotl Sp8 loss-of-function, embryonic AER Sp/Fgf8 genetics, and other mouse “regeneration” programs (Lin28a, MRL ear holes, OSK, GDF11/parabiosis, Levin BioDome, peptide SKUs) that are not the 2026 AAV (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; sections below).

## What the words mean

💯 Adult laboratory mice replace a multi-tissue digit tip after amputation through the distal third phalanx (P3) if enough nail organ remains; a more proximal cut through mid-P2 or the P3 base does not grow a patterned digit back (Borgens, Science, 1982, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7100922, PMID 7100922; Neufeld and Zhao, Wound Repair Regen, 1995, https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1524-475x.1995.30410.x, PMID 17147657; Dawson et al., Wound Repair Regen, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1111/wrr.12856, PMID 32815252; Seifert and Muneoka, Developmental Biology, 2018, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2017.08.007; Takeo et al., Nature, 2013, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12214, PMID 23760480).

📚 “Replicate salamander regeneration in mice,” as asked, means whole-limb epimorphosis after a proximal amputation (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026, significance line vs Wake Forest News, 2026).

📚 The technical object under the 2026 paper is partial rescue or acceleration of the mouse’s existing P3 program (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 A “salamander gene” in the headlines is mouse/human SP6 and SP8 (and axolotl Sp8), not an axolotl-only transgene inserted into the mouse (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; Wake Forest News, 2026).

📚 “Limb” in the press is an arm or leg; “limb” in the mouse experiments is a distal phalanx past a regeneration-competent plane (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; Dawson et al., Wound Repair Regen, 2021).

📚 A blastema is a transient proliferative mesenchyme under a wound epidermis / apical epithelial cap / regeneration epidermis (RE) that rebuilds the missing pattern — the operational difference between P3 epimorphosis and a collagen cap (Seifert and Muneoka, Developmental Biology, 2018, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2017.08.007; Storer and Miller, Open Biology, 2020, https://doi.org/10.1098/rsob.200194, PMID 32993414).

📚 The apical ectodermal ridge (AER) is the embryonic limb-bud epithelium that secretes FGF8; the regeneration epidermis is the post-amputation epithelium over a blastema — analogous FGF job, different structure and timing (Kawakami et al., Development, 2004, https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.01331, PMID 15358670; Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 LEN is a zebrafish tissue-regeneration enhancer element (TREE) upstream of leptin b: injury-gated, not a developmental AER enhancer, and not an axolotl sequence (Kang et al., Nature, 2016, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17644, PMID 27049946).

📚 AAV.cc47 is an AAV9-derived capsid evolved by iterative cycling in mice, pigs, and macaques; Gonzalez et al. reported higher potency than AAV9 for reporter, Cre, and CRISPR cargos (Gonzalez et al., Nature Communications, 2022, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-33745-4, PMID 36216836).

## Mechanism

📚 Epidermal Sp6/Sp8 homologs are induced in the regeneration epidermis of zebrafish caudal fin, axolotl mid-zeugopod limb, and mouse P3 digit tip, and are undetectable after non-regenerative mouse P2 amputation (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; zebrafish integrated scRNA-seq atlas N=17,449 cells with Dre.sp6/sp8a/sp8b enriched in intermediate epithelium peaking 2–3 dpa; mouse ISH/IF on P3 at 7–14 dpa vs P2 7–14 dpa; axolotl HCR WMFISH at mid radius/ulna 7/10/14 dpa peaking 10 dpa).

📚 Basal-epidermis inducible knockout of mouse Sp6, Sp8, or both impairs P3 bony digit-tip regeneration; the double cKO nearly abolishes distal bone beyond the amputation plane and leaves a blunt ossified stump rather than a delayed full regenerate (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; K14-CreER; Sp6fl/fl and/or Sp8fl/fl adults 2–6 mo both sexes, tamoxifen 75 mg/kg IP ×5 d then 5 d rest; 28 dpa control n=11, each cKO n=5: total bone length −13% Sp6 / −19% Sp8 / −21% double vs tamoxifen WT; 56 dpa control n=7, Sp6 n=5, Sp8 n=4, double n=4: double-cKO total length −28%, total volume −50%, regenerate length −40%, regenerate volume −68%).

📚 Mouse Sp6+Sp8 cKO P3 regenerates at 14 dpa (peak blastema) showed an IL-17 / osteoclast RNA-seq signature (Il17a, Rorc, Cxcl5, Mmp9, Oscar, Il1b) and more Cathepsin K+ cells (Sp6 cKO vs control p=0.0034) (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 Axolotl Sp8 CRISPR knockouts develop patterned limbs but fail to correctly pattern distal skeletal elements after mid-radius/ulna amputation in a minority of animals; secondary regenerates degrade further (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; leucistic d/d larval axolotls 3–10 cm snout–cloaca, F0 crispants and germline F1 Amex.Sp8 KO vs Amex.Tyr gRNA; ~26–34% of animals with regeneration-specific mis-patterning; N behind that rate is not stated beyond “across independent gRNAs”; primary 35 dpa, secondary harvest ~115 d after primary, imaged 135 d post-secondary).

📚 That axolotl result is loss of a native salamander factor, not a mouse gain-of-function and not the AAV result (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 Zebrafish Dre.sp6, Dre.sp8a, or Dre.sp8b single deletions, and the tested double-deletion combinations that survived, did not produce a detectable caudal-fin regeneration defect; Dre.sp8a; Dre.sp8b doubles died before maturity (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; N=8–10 fish/group, 4 rays/fish for singles; no change in fraction regenerated length at 4/7/14 dpa; EK zebrafish 3–12 mo).

📚 In embryonic limb ectoderm, Sp6 and Sp8 sit downstream of Wnt/β-catenin and are required for Fgf8 activation in the AER: loss of all four alleles, or retention of a single Sp6 allele, yields tetra-amelia with no Fgf8 induction (Haro et al., PLoS Genet, 2014, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1004468, PMID 25166858; N=3/102 newborns Sp6−/−;Sp8−/− and 10/102 Sp6+/−;Sp8−/−, both 100% tetra-amelic; 11/102 Sp6−/−;Sp8+/− SHFM/bidorsal tips).

📚 Lin et al. mapped a WNT–SP8–FGF8 cassette shared by limb and genital-tubercle outgrowth; Sp8 mutants phenocopy WNT/FGF appendage loss (Lin et al., PLOS Genetics, 2013, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1003231, PMID 23349633).

📚 Talamillo et al. showed Sp6/epiprofin loss yields syndactyly and AER defects (Talamillo et al., Developmental Biology, 2010, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2009.11.020, PMID 19913007).

📚 Chick overexpression and dominant-negative plus zebrafish morpholino experiments place Sp8/Sp9 as positive regulators of Fgf8 in the AER (Kawakami et al., Development, 2004, https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.01331, PMID 15358670).

📚 Those AER papers are embryonic limb budding, not adult appendage gene therapy (Haro et al., 2014; Lin et al., 2013; Talamillo et al., 2010; Kawakami et al., 2004).

📚 Wnt activity in the nail epithelium is required for mouse P3 regeneration: K14-Cre β-catenin deletion blocks nail and bone regrowth; more-proximal amputations that spare nail-stem-cell territory can be pushed by epithelial β-catenin stabilization (Takeo et al., Nature, 2013, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12214, PMID 23760480).

📚 Kang et al. identified LEN as a TREE that opens after injury, drives injury-dependent expression from minimal promoters, is active in injured neonatal mouse tissues, and can modulate zebrafish fin regeneration when linked to Fgf effectors (Kang et al., Nature, 2016, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17644, PMID 27049946).

📚 Deleting LEN abolished detectable induction of nearby leptin b during zebrafish fin regeneration (Thompson et al., Development, 2020, https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.191262, PMID 32665240).

📚 Zebrafish TREEs packaged in systemically delivered AAV directed injury-site transgene expression in adult mouse and pig heart and carried a constitutively active YAP cargo that improved post-MI function — the same enhancer-AAV logic later used for LEN–Fgf8 in digits (Yan et al., Cell Stem Cell, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2022.11.012, PMID 36538932; Asokan is an author).

📚 The 2026 cargo is LEN–Hsp68–Fgf8–P2A–GFP in AAV.cc47, given retro-orbitally at 2.5×10^13 vg/kg at 10 dpa — mouse Fgf8 under a zebrafish enhancer, not an axolotl transgene (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 LEN-driven luciferase in AAV.cc47 after 10 dpa systemic injection stayed at the injured P3 tip for ~10 days and was undetectable in other tissues except liver (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; N=4; 2.5×10^13 vg/kg; IVIS at 12–35 dpa).

🥼 Whether the mouse digit blastema is a bag of lineage-restricted progenitors or an environmentally induced mesenchymal state is a live paper fight: Rinkevich/Lehoczky report lineage restriction; Storer et al. report Dmp1-lineage osteoblasts/osteocytes contributing ~26% of Pdgfra+ blastema cells and contributing to dermis (Rinkevich et al., Nature, 2011, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10346; Lehoczky et al., PNAS, 2011, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1118017108; Storer et al., Developmental Cell, 2020, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2019.12.004).

## Animal data

📚 Amputation through the proximal 20% of mouse terminal phalanx precluded nail-plate regrowth and bone grew minimally or regressed; cuts in the distal 40% usually attained or surpassed original length by 5 weeks in adults and neonates (Neufeld and Zhao, Wound Repair Regen, 1995, https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1524-475x.1995.30410.x, PMID 17147657).

📚 Complete patterned extremity regeneration in adult mice is restricted to distal P3; competence attenuates at more proximal P3 planes and fails as a full regenerate (Dawson et al., Wound Repair Regen, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1111/wrr.12856, PMID 32815252).

📚 Brown et al. amputated through the distal 2/3 of P3 with ≥half the nail matrix retained, or through mid-P2 as a non-regenerative plane, in adults 2–6 months of both sexes (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 AAV.cc47 LEN–Fgf8, given retro-orbitally at 10 dpa, confined GFP to distal epidermis at 14 dpa and partially rescued Sp6+Sp8 cKO P3 bone versus LEN–GFP: +32% total bone volume and +24% regenerated bone length at 28 dpa (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; N=6/group; 2.5×10^13 vg/kg; the same sentence asserts greater regenerated bone volume but does not give that percentage).

📚 That rescue is versus GFP controls in the double cKO, not a return to wild-type bone or to an axolotl limb (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 The same vector accelerated wild-type P3 regeneration: +34% bony regenerate volume at 21 dpa versus LEN–GFP, a midpoint chosen to ask whether FGF8 expedites the normal program (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; N=12/group; CD-1 adults 2–6 mo both sexes).

📚 LEN–Fgf8 did not restore regeneration after mid-P2 amputation; GFP was largely absent from the P2 distal wound epidermis (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; N=12/group; 21 dpa micro-CT + GFP).

📚 A 2025 PSRC meeting abstract from the same Duke group reported LEN-driven FGF8 AAV accelerating wild-type distal-phalangeal regeneration and partially rescuing “FGF8 pathway knockout” digits, with LEN localizing to the distal tip after systemic injection and luciferase lasting ≥10 days; the abstract says ongoing work aims to enhance regeneration from more proximal limb amputation sites (Brown lab, Plast Reconstr Surg Glob Open, 2025, https://doi.org/10.1097/01.gox.0001112232.83563.36, PMC12023080).

📚 That abstract is the same Duke / Asokan program, not an independent lab replication; the knockout genotype is named differently than the PNAS Sp6/Sp8 cKO (Brown lab, PRS Global Open, 2025; Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 iLin28a transgenic mice enhanced neonatal digit-tip connective-tissue and bone regrowth, adult ear-punch repair, and hair cycling via metabolic reprogramming — a different mouse regeneration story, not SP/FGF8 AAV (Shyh-Chang et al., Cell, 2013, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2013.09.059, PMID 24209617).

📚 MRL/MpJ (and MRL/lpr) close a ~2 mm through-and-through ear hole in <4 weeks with cartilage/hair return; C57BL/6 holes persist; MRL 4-week hole size ~0–0.4 mm versus open B6 holes (Clark, Clark, and Heber-Katz, Clinical Immunology and Immunopathology, 1998, https://doi.org/10.1006/clin.1998.4519, PMID 9683548; McBrearty et al., PNAS, 1998, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.95.20.11792, PMID 9751744).

🥼 Whether MRL ear-hole closure is epimorphic regeneration or accelerated/hyper-fibrotic repair is a live paper fight: Bedelbaeva et al. reported p21−/− ear holes closing like MRL by day 28 versus open B6/B6129 holes; Colwell et al. reported MRL/MpJ skin wounds healing with scar; Robey and Murry reported no MRL heart regeneration after infarct or cryoinjury (Bedelbaeva et al., PNAS, 2010, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1000830107; Colwell et al., Wound Repair Regen, 2006, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1524-475X.2005.00092.x, PMID 16476076; Robey and Murry, Cardiovasc Pathol, 2008, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.carpath.2007.01.005, PMID 18160055).

📚 AAV2 OSK (no MYC) restored mouse RGC methylation/transcriptome and vision after crush or glaucoma; cyclic OSKM shifted clocks in old 4F mice — axon/vision and systemic reprogramming, not a digit blastema (Lu et al., Nature, 2020, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2975-4, PMID 33268865; already filed at [03-epigenetic-alterations](../../hallmarks/03-epigenetic-alterations/report.md)).

📚 Heterochronic parabiosis for 5 weeks restored aged satellite-cell Delta/Notch and muscle regeneration (GFP+ myotubes <0.1%; n≥3 pairs; young 2–3 mo / old 19–26 mo) (Conboy et al., Nature, 2005, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03260, PMID 15716955; already filed at [09-stem-cell-exhaustion](../../hallmarks/09-stem-cell-exhaustion/report.md)).

🥼 Whether GDF11 is a declining youth factor that restores heart/muscle or a myostatin-like inhibitor is a live paper fight, already written at [10-altered-intercellular-communication](../../hallmarks/10-altered-intercellular-communication/report.md) (Loffredo et al., Cell, 2013, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2013.04.015, PMID 23663781; Sinha et al., Science, 2014, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1251152, PMID 24797481; Egerman et al., Cell Metab, 2015, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2015.05.010, PMID 26001423).

📚 A 24-hour BioDome plus five-drug silk gel (1,4-DPCA 0.014 µg/ml, BDNF 0.5 µg/ml, GH 0.5 µg/ml, resolvin D5 0.036 µg/ml, RA 0.015 µg/ml) on adult female Xenopus (n=115 across no-device / BioDome-only / MDT) produced 18-month patterned hindlimb outgrowth with more vessels and acetylated-tubulin+ nerves versus hypomorphic spikes (Murugan et al., Science Advances, 2022, https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abj2164, PMID 35080981; MDT nerve n=10 vs BioDome n=8 vs no-device n=6).

🤔 Levin said mouse tests were next; Science News reported the lab had “moved on to similar work in mice” with the same and new cocktails — that is interview status, not a published mouse BioDome limb paper (Murugan et al., 2022; Science News, https://www.sciencenews.org/article/frog-leg-regeneration-new-device-regrow-amputation; Tufts Now, https://now.tufts.edu/2022/01/26/scientists-regrow-frogs-lost-leg).

## Human data

📚 Children can regrow a fingertip distal to the DIP / first joint under conservative dressing if the nail matrix is not destroyed; Illingworth described nonintervention regeneration, including a 5-year-old complete nail-bearing tip in ~3 months (Illingworth, Journal of Pediatric Surgery, 1974, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-3468(74)80220-4, PMID 4473530; Douglas, Australian Paediatric Journal, 1972, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5074173/, PMID 5074173).

📚 Illingworth/Barker recorded injury currents on 10 children’s amputated fingertips peaking at mean 22 µA/cm² around day 8, similar in form to salamander stump currents (Barker, Jaffe, and Illingworth, Clinical Physics and Physiological Measurement, 1980, https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0815/1/1/007).

📚 That is a limited human P3 / nail-bed program, not a therapy that copied salamanders into mice (Illingworth, 1974; Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 ClinicalTrials.gov queried 3 Sep 2026 for FGF8 / SP6 / SP8 gene therapy for human limb or digit regeneration returned no matching interventional records; FGF hits in adjacent searches are FGF18 sprifermin cartilage (NCT01066871), rhFGF-2 periodontal (NCT02337166), and FGF1 spinal-cord device (NCT02490501), not SP/FGF8 limb AAV (ClinicalTrials.gov, https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?term=FGF8+gene+therapy; https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?term=FGF8+OR+SP6+OR+SP8+limb+regeneration+digit+gene+therapy).

📚 FGF8 mRNA was present in malignant prostatic epithelium in 85/106 (80.2%) prostate cancers and high expression correlated with Gleason (P=0.0004), stage (P=0.0016), and worse survival (P=0.034) — an association in existing tumors, not a demonstrated harm of LEN–FGF8 digit AAV, which has not been given to humans (Dorkin et al., Oncogene, 1999, https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.onc.1202624, PMID 10348350; N=106 cancers + 10 BPH).

📚 LEN–FGF8 / SP gene therapy has not been given to humans in any compiled interventional record; Dorkin 1999 is a tumor-association series, not this vector (ClinicalTrials.gov, 3 Sep 2026; Dorkin et al., Oncogene, 1999).

## Measurement

📚 Standard mouse P3 endpoints in this literature are micro-CT total/regenerate bone length and volume, histology of blastema versus collagen cap, and nail outgrowth (Seifert and Muneoka, Developmental Biology, 2018; Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 Brown et al. used Nikon XTH 225 ST at 7 µm voxels and BoneJ volume, scored at 21/28/56 dpa, with histolysis then blastema around 14 dpa (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026, methods).

📚 Expression readouts in the 2026 paper are zebrafish scRNA-seq, mouse ISH/IF, and axolotl HCR whole-mount FISH; vector tropism is GFP histology plus IVIS luciferase (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 MRL-style assays score ear-hole diameter over ~4 weeks (Clark et al., 1998; Bedelbaeva et al., 2010).

📚 Xenopus BioDome work scored 18-month paddle morphology, vessels, and nerves (Murugan et al., Science Advances, 2022).

🤔 Clinic “regeneration” scores for peptide SKUs are pain, tendon anecdotes, and storefront copy — not P3 micro-CT and not a blastema (Mindful Research / happypeptides.com catalog pull 3 Sep 2026; already filed at [bpc-157](../../compounds/bpc-157/report.md), [tb-500](../../compounds/tb-500/report.md), [ghk-cu](../../compounds/ghk-cu/report.md)).

## What clinics and self-experimenters are doing

🤔 No LEN–Fgf8 AAV.cc47 or named “salamander FGF” SKU appears on the clinic/forum map compiled here; the regeneration vials that are for sale are peptide SKUs (PeptideProbe, https://peptideprobe.com/dosage-charts/bpc-157; Mindful Research / happypeptides.com catalog pull 3 Sep 2026).

🤔 Mindful Research (happypeptides.com) lists BPC-157 10 mg vial $44, TB-500 10 mg $68, GHK-Cu 50–100 mg $42–79.50, plus GLOW/KLOW blends, as research-chem “regeneration” SKUs; Mindful Research sells the vials (already filed: [bpc-157](../../compounds/bpc-157/report.md), [tb-500](../../compounds/tb-500/report.md), [ghk-cu](../../compounds/ghk-cu/report.md); catalog pull 3 Sep 2026).

🤔 Peptide-protocol blogs print BPC-157 practice at ~200–500 µg SC 1–2× daily, 4–8 weeks on / 2–4 weeks off, often near a tendon, sold as tissue-regeneration (PeptideProbe, https://peptideprobe.com/dosage-charts/bpc-157; Peptide Dosing Protocols, https://www.peptidedosingprotocols.com/protocol/bpc-157).

🤔 GHK-Cu clinic/wiki practice is topical 0.01–1% and subQ 1–2 mg/day, 4–8 weeks on (already filed: [ghk-cu](../../compounds/ghk-cu/report.md); [13-extracellular-matrix-changes](../../hallmarks/13-extracellular-matrix-changes/report.md)).

🤼 Peptide storefronts and protocol blogs treat BPC-157 / TB-500 / GHK-Cu as regeneration products; those SKUs are not LEN–FGF8 AAV and are not SP genetics ([bpc-157](../../compounds/bpc-157/report.md); PeptideProbe, 2026).

🤔 Morphoceuticals (Levin/Kaplan; Juvenescence + Prime Movers Lab $8M seed, 15 Feb 2023) sells the company story as “limb regeneration is not science fiction” from the frog BioDome, not the 2026 PNAS AAV (Morphoceuticals PR, https://www.morphoceuticals.com/21523).

🤔 Conservative pediatric fingertip dressings remain clinical lore from the Illingworth/Douglas series, not a biohacker product (Illingworth, 1974).

## Speculative

🐉 Whole-limb replacement in a mammal from the 2026 vector is a leap: the paper’s own P2 arm did not regenerate, and the authors call mammalian complex-structure instruction unsolved (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026, discussion; no source beyond that inference).

🐉 Unpublished mouse BioDome / electroceutical cocktails as the same product as LEN–FGF8 AAV collapses two mechanisms (Murugan et al., 2022 interviews; Morphoceuticals, 2023; no source that they are one program).

🐉 Press “replace prosthetics with living limbs” as an endpoint of P3 bone µCT is a hope stacked on a digit-tip midpoint, not a result (ScienceDaily, 2026; Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

## What is actually on the table

📚 A three-lab 2026 PNAS paper shows a conserved epidermal SP6/SP8 module in fish fin, axolotl limb, and mouse P3, necessity of those factors for mouse P3 bone and (partially) axolotl distal pattern, and an injury-gated zebrafish-LEN FGF8 AAV that partially rescues SP-null P3 bone (+32% volume / +24% regenerate length vs GFP, n=6) and accelerates wild-type P3 bone (+34% regenerate volume at 21 dpa, n=12) (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

📚 The same vector does not convert a P2 amputation into a regenerate (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; n=12).

📚 Zebrafish SP knockouts did not show a fin-regeneration phenotype, so genetic necessity of the module is not shown in fish (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026).

⛔ The slogan that mice now do what salamanders do — grow a whole limb because a salamander gene was copied in — does not match the payload, the plane, or the authors’ own “unsolved problem” line (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; Wake Forest News, 2026; ScienceDaily, 2026).

🥼 Digit-blastema potency, MRL-as-regeneration, and GDF11-as-youth-factor remain paper fights in adjacent literatures; they are not the 2026 AAV result (Rinkevich 2011 / Storer 2020; Bedelbaeva 2010 / Clark 1998; hallmark 10).

🤼 Press “holy grail limbs” versus Fight Aging “modest digit-tip,” and peptide-shop “regeneration” versus the PNAS vector, are amateur/slogan fights, not lab replications (ScienceDaily, 2026; Fight Aging, 2026; PeptideProbe, 2026).

🤔 People are buying BPC-157 / TB-500 / GHK-Cu vials and reading Morphoceuticals frog-limb copy; nobody is offering LEN–FGF8 AAV (Mindful Research, 2026; Morphoceuticals, 2023).

🐉 Whole-limb mammal replacement from this vector, and collapsing Levin mice with Brown digits, are still guesses (Brown et al., PNAS, 2026; Murugan et al., 2022).
